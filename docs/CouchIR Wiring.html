CouchIR Wiring

Everything that connects to the little blue Arduino board behind the TV: what each part does, how they work together, and where every wire goes.

The parts and their jobs

Leonardo board

the brain · already working

Plugged into the TV PC by USB. The cable powers it and carries its messages. With ErP disabled in the BIOS, it stays powered even while the PC is asleep or off.

IR sensor

the ear · already working

Hears the phone's IR blaster (and other remotes) and passes every button to the board. Nothing about it changes except where its power wire plugs in.

Relay module

two fingers · new

Two electric switches. Relay K1 "presses" the PC's power button, relay K2 the TV's, for a third of a second each time. You'll hear a soft click.

Mini breadboard

a power strip · new

The board has only one 5 V socket, but the sensor and the relay module both need 5 V. The breadboard shares that one socket between them.

Resistors + old USB cable

an eye on the TV · optional

The TV's power button only toggles, so the board needs to know whether the TV is already on. The TV's USB port has power only while the TV is on, and the two resistors let the board read that safely.

ESP32

Wi-Fi ear · arriving later

Lets the Oppo, which has no IR blaster, press Power over Wi-Fi. It isn't part of today's wiring; pins 0 and 1 are kept free for it.

How it works

You press Power, everything is off

  1. The sensor hears it and tells the board.
  2. The board wakes the PC: over USB if it's asleep, or with relay K1 if it's fully off or after a power cut.
  3. Relay K2 presses the TV's button, so the TV comes on too.

You press Power while watching

  1. The board passes it to CouchTV, which puts the PC to sleep.
  2. The board notices the PC's USB go quiet.
  3. A few seconds later, relay K2 switches the TV off.

The PC sleeps or wakes by itself

  1. Sleep timer, idle, Windows' menu: the PC goes to sleep.
  2. The board notices, and K2 turns the TV off.
  3. When the PC wakes, K2 turns the TV back on.

What we do next

  1. At the laptop. Make wires 3 to 8 (the IR sensor stays as it is apart from wire 3). Plug the board into the laptop. Claude puts firmware version 4 on it and clicks each relay once to check.
  2. Find each relay's NO terminal. In the same session, two spare jumpers go on for a moment: relay K1's middle screw to a board GND, one outer screw to pin 10. The board reports whether that terminal is NO. Then the same for K2.
  3. At the TV PC. Plug the board back in by USB. Screw the PC button's two wires into K1 (COM + NO) and the TV button's into K2. Press Power on the phone to test.
  4. Check the TV's USB port. Plug a phone into it: it should charge only while the TV is on. If it does, add wires 11 to 16 and switch sensing on. If not, skip TV sensing; it works without it.
  5. When the ESP32 arrives. It connects to pins 0 and 1, and the Oppo gets a Power button that works over Wi-Fi.
