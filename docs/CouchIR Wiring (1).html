The parts and their jobs

Leonardo board

the brain

Plugged into the TV PC by USB, which powers the whole box and carries its messages. With ErP disabled in the BIOS, it stays powered while the PC is asleep or off, so it can wake it.

IR sensor

the ear · TSOP38238

Hears the phone's IR blaster and any other remote, and passes every button to the Leonardo.

Relay module

two fingers · 2 channels

Two electric switches wired across the real buttons. Relay 1 presses the PC's power button for 0.3 s, relay 2 the TV's for 0.4 s. You hear a soft click.

ESP32 board

Wi-Fi for the box · 30-pin DevKit

On your Wi-Fi all the time, so any paired phone can press Power, even the Oppo with no IR blaster, and see whether the PC and TV are on. It only obeys signed requests.

Mini breadboard

junctions

Shares the Leonardo's single 5 V socket among four parts, holds the ground connections, and carries the small resistor circuits.

Old USB cable + 2 resistors

an eye on the TV · 2.2 kΩ, 10 kΩ

The TV's power button only toggles. Its USB port has 5 V only while the TV is on, so the Leonardo reads that port to know the TV's real state.

3 resistors + a capacitor

for the ESP32 · 3 × 2.2 kΩ, 470 µF

The resistors turn the Leonardo's 5 V signal into the 3.3 V the ESP32 can take. The capacitor steadies the 5 V when the ESP32's Wi-Fi draws bursts of current.

How it works

Power, with everything off

  1. The IR sensor hears it, or a phone sends it to the ESP32 over Wi-Fi.
  2. The Leonardo wakes the PC over USB if it's asleep, or presses its button with relay 1 if it's fully off.
  3. Relay 2 switches the TV on, and TV sensing checks it really came on.

Power, while watching

  1. The Leonardo passes it to CouchTV, which puts the PC to sleep.
  2. CouchTV tells the Leonardo first, so relay 2 switches the TV off straight away.

The PC sleeps or wakes by itself

  1. Sleep timer, idle, Windows' menu: the PC's USB goes quiet, and within 4 s the TV goes off.
  2. When the PC wakes, the TV comes back on.
  3. The TV ignores its button for about 15 s after switching, so the box always waits that long between TV presses.

Who may press Power over Wi-Fi

  • CouchTV makes a secret key and gives it to the ESP32 through the Leonardo's USB, every time it connects.
  • Paired phones get the same key from CouchTV, and sign every request with it.
  • Neighbours on your Wi-Fi can find the box, but it ignores them.

The boards' software and settings

Leonardo · CouchIR 7

firmware/CouchIR
  • Decodes almost any IR remote and reports each button to CouchTV.
  • Wakes the PC over USB, or presses its button when it's fully off.
  • Keeps the TV in step with the PC, watching the PC's USB and the TV's USB port.
  • Passes CouchTV's key to the ESP32, and Power from the ESP32 to the PC.
  • Keeps a log of its last 60 events, to diagnose power problems.

ESP32 · CouchLink 2

firmware/CouchLink
  • Stays on your Wi-Fi, so phones can press Power and see whether the PC and TV are on.
  • Obeys only requests signed with the key; its challenge changes every minute and after each Power, so old requests can't be replayed.
  • Its web page shows only its version and whether it has the key: no Power button.
  • To change its Wi-Fi, hold BOOT for 3 s while it's running: the blue light blinks and a network called CouchTV-Setup opens for 10 minutes. It only joins 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.

CouchTV gives the box its key every time it connects to the Leonardo. Forget phones (F8 on the TV's Remote screen) makes a new key, so a lost phone can no longer press Power.

Flashing the boards again

  1. Bring the box to the laptop. Plug the Leonardo's USB straight into the laptop, not the hub the keyboard and mouse use: that port has twice failed to start the Leonardo. It shows up as "USB Serial Device".
  2. Then the ESP32's own micro-USB. Both can be plugged in at once. It shows up as "Silicon Labs CP210x". Don't hold BOOT while plugging it in: that starts its flashing mode instead of the firmware.
  3. Claude flashes them with the Arduino command-line tools: the Leonardo keeps its settings and log, the ESP32 keeps its Wi-Fi and key. If a board doesn't show up, try another port or cable; some cables only charge.
  4. Put the box back. Only the Leonardo's USB goes to the TV PC. When CouchTV starts, it gives the ESP32 its key again.

If something's off

Power by phone does nothing, but IR works

Open http://192.168.0.14 on the phone. If it doesn't open, the ESP32 is off your Wi-Fi: hold BOOT for 3 s and set it up again. If it says "Key from the TV: not yet", restart CouchTV, which gives the key when it connects to the receiver. Then open the app once while the TV PC is on, so the phone gets the key too.

The box keeps restarting

The PC's standby USB power isn't enough for it all, and the Leonardo's start count (BOOTS) jumps. Power the box from a phone charger instead of the PC.

The TV is on when it should be off

Press the TV's own power button once. With TV sensing on, the box sees the TV's real state and carries on from there.

The TV ignores a press

It ignores its button for about 15 seconds after switching. The box waits for that by itself; pressing again sooner doesn't help.

The board's page opens, but the app can't tell if the PC and TV are on

The ESP32 can't reach the Leonardo. Check that TX2 goes to pin 0, that pin 1 goes to column A, and that both resistors and RX2 share column B on the same side of the middle gap.
